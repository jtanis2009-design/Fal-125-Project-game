## Which real prehistoric animals have features that suggest interesting enemy abilities?

### Takeaway
Many real animals have a single striking, well-documented feature that turns neatly into one turn-based mechanic: giant claws (Therizinosaurus), a thick bone dome (Pachycephalosaurus), a sound-making crest (Parasaurolophus), four iridescent wings (Microraptor), palate teeth that stop prey escaping (mosasaurs), and a display sail (Dimetrodon). Several popular "facts" are movie myths (Dilophosaurus venom and frill), and that gap itself can be a theme: Fal-125 "improved" the animal into the myth.

### Cited Findings
- **Therizinosaurus**: hand claws are the longest known for any land animal, over 50 cm (estimates of the full length vary, up to about 70 cm or more). They were probably for pulling down plants rather than attack; it was likely a plant-eater. — [Therizinosaurus (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therizinosaurus); [Gondwana Studios](https://www.gondwanastudios.com/info/ther.htm)
- **Pachycephalosaurus**: a solid bone dome sometimes over 20 cm thick. A 2013 study (Peterson et al.) found that about one fifth of domes have lesions consistent with bone infection from injury, which supports fighting. Others argue they hit each other's flanks rather than head-on. A structural study says head-butting was possible at low speeds. — [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/paleontology/science-head-butting-dome-headed-dinosaurs-01247.html); [ResearchGate study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228693456_Structural_mechanics_of_Pachycephalosaur_crania_permitted_head-butting_behavior); [Extinct Atlas](https://extinctatlas.org/species-guide/pachycephalosaurus-head-butting-behavior/)
- **Parasaurolophus**: a long, hollow, backward-curving crest connected to the nasal passages; most evidence points to sound and communication (deep, far-carrying calls). — [dino-world.com](https://dino-world.com/8-dinosaurs-that-evolution-made-almost-too-strange-to-believe-1-78498/)
- **Dilophosaurus**: venom spit and neck frill are fiction (from the 1990 novel and the film); no fossil evidence. The double crest is real (the name means "two-crested lizard"), probably for display. The real animal was much bigger than the film's, up to about 7 m. — [Natural History Museum](https://nhm.ac.uk/discover/debunking-dinosaur-myths-and-movie-misconceptions.html); [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/jurassic-park-got-almost-everything-wrong-about-iconic-dinosaur-dilophosaurus); [NHM Dino Directory](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/dino-directory/Dilophosaurus.html)
- **Microraptor**: four wings (flight feathers on arms AND legs); fossil melanosomes show it was iridescent (the first known iridescent dinosaur). Whether it truly flew or only glided is debated (the "biplane" model). — [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/science/article/2012-03/reconstructed-dinosaur-four-iridescent-wings/); [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/microraptor-the-dinosaur-that-flew-like-a-biplane); [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/science/paleontology/10-feathered-facts-about-microraptor)
- **Carnotaurus**: shorter arms than any other large meat-eater, possibly vestigial; a pair of short horns over the eyes; everything known comes from one skeleton; about 7.5-8 m long. (Mostly kids' sites; treat numbers loosely.) — [Carnotaurus (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnotaurus); [Active Wild](https://www.activewild.com/carnotaurus-facts-information/)
- **Troodon / troodontids**: popularly called "the smartest dinosaur" because of a large brain-to-body ratio; small, bird-like; tooth shape suggests it may have been omnivorous. The "smart" idea is mostly speculation in pop culture. — [Troodontidae (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troodontidae); [Active Wild](https://www.activewild.com/popular-dinosaurs/)
- **Mosasaurs**: marine reptiles (not dinosaurs); Mosasaurus hoffmannii possibly about 13 m. A second set of teeth on the roof of the mouth (pterygoid teeth) helped grip prey so it could not escape; flexible, snake-like jaws are often described. Whether they "ratcheted" prey down is debated and varies by species. — [Mosasaurinae (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosasaurinae); [UC Magazine](https://magazine.uc.edu/editors_picks/recent_features/mosasaur1.html); [Mosasaur (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosasaur)
- **Dimetrodon**: a synapsid (closer to mammals), NOT a dinosaur; an apex predator; the sail's job is debated (temperature control vs display; recent work favours display). — [Dimetrodon (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimetrodon)
- **Pterosaurs (e.g. Quetzalcoatlus)**: a quadrupedal (four-limb) launch has been proposed for the largest pterosaurs; a 2022 study showed that a water launch was possible for small ones. — [PMC 2022 study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9023563/)

## What de-extinction / genetic-modification horror ideas recur, and which feel fresh?

### Takeaway
The standard ideas are: gaps in old DNA filled with DNA from living animals (the Jurassic Park idea), control by collars or implants, imprinting, and clones with defects. Real 2025 news gives a fresh, grounded angle: Colossal's "dire wolves" are gray wolves with 20 edits on 14 genes, and scientists say they "look like" the extinct animal but are not it. That fits FAL125 very well: Fal-125's "dinosaurs" are really modern animals edited to look the part, the same way Sunshine and Nukasourus are lizards with dinosaur DNA.

### Cited Findings
- Colossal Biosciences edited 20 sites on 14 genes in a modern gray wolf genome. Critics call the pups gray wolves with some dire-wolf-like traits ("a gray wolf clone with some mutations"). — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-scientific-debate-over-colossals-de-extinct-dire-wolves/); [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/animals/extinct-species/our-animals-are-gray-wolves-colossal-didnt-de-extinct-dire-wolves-chief-scientist-clarifies)
- Colossal's chief scientist said: "Our animals are gray wolves." The company then used the term "functional de-extinction." The IUCN canid expert group rejected the claim that they are dire wolves. — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/animals/extinct-species/our-animals-are-gray-wolves-colossal-didnt-de-extinct-dire-wolves-chief-scientist-clarifies); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-scientific-debate-over-colossals-de-extinct-dire-wolves/)
- There are more than 12 million base-pair differences between dire and gray wolves; the edits affected only one copy of each gene; no peer-reviewed paper existed at first. — [Why Evolution Is True](https://whyevolutionistrue.com/2025/04/13/colossal-biosciences-responds-to-criticism-of-the-dire-wolf-de-extinction-but-not-convincingly/); [Earth.com](https://earth.com/news/scientists-no-those-are-definitely-not-resurrected-dire-wolves-grey-wolf-genes)
- Conservation critics worry that de-extinction hype makes people think extinction can be undone, which takes attention away from living species. — [Living with Wolves](https://www.livingwithwolves.org/the-illusion-of-de-extinction-colossal-biosciences-and-the-myth-of-the-dire-wolf/); [Discover Wildlife](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/dire-wolf-de-extinction)
- The Jurassic Park novel's Dilophosaurus "venom" shows the classic fiction pattern: the made creature gets abilities the real animal never had. — [Natural History Museum](https://nhm.ac.uk/discover/debunking-dinosaur-myths-and-movie-misconceptions.html)
- Lab-made dinosaurs are an old horror idea (*Carnosaur*), and *Dino Crisis* puts dinosaurs inside a research facility. — [Dread Central: Carnosaur](https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/489598/a-history-of-carnosaur-the-ultimate-record-of-dino-horror-new-blood-drops/); [Dread Central: Dino Crisis](https://dreadcentral.com/editorials/520904/over-25-years-later-dino-crisis-still-rules)

### Inferences (ideas; Claude's drafts, not canon)
**Recurring and well-worn (use carefully; very tied to Jurassic Park):** frog DNA filling the gaps; "life finds a way" breeding; all-female control; a lysine contingency; raptors testing fences; the corporate tour video. Avoid these specific ones.

**Fresh angles that fit "successful = obedient, failed = can think":**
- **"Costume DNA" (from the real dire wolf debate)**: Fal-125's dinosaurs are modern animals (lizards, birds, crocodiles, even humans) edited to LOOK prehistoric. Archive line idea: "Resemblance: 94%. Resemblance is enough." It explains why Sunshine and Nukasourus exist and makes every enemy a sad copy.
- **Obedience as the defect**: the "successful" ones had the thinking part edited out on purpose. Mechanic: enemies act in a perfect, predictable order (show the next move as an intent icon). An enemy that "breaks obedience" (a rare variant) acts randomly and is the scary one; it could be a rare unlock, like Clown's rare boss forms.
- **The Ringer (name pun)**: a "ringer" = a look-alike substitute, AND a bell that commands. Idea: a control tone. Enemies with a "Collar" status obey a "Ring" sound: when the bell rings (every N rounds, a World 2 rule), all collared enemies act at once. Breaking a collar (a strong hit on it) makes that enemy confused / attack its own side for 1 turn. NOTE: World 2 already has "the Tide" world special (V269); a bell could be an extra boss rule rather than a replacement. Ask the author.
- **Imprinting**: a hatchling-type enemy imprints on the first fighter that hits it and follows that fighter (attacks only them, or later defends them). Could be a recruit or a pet idea.
- **Patchwork filler**: each enemy has a hidden "filler" type (what animal filled the DNA gaps) shown in its card: "Filler: monitor lizard / crow / human". The filler gives a small weakness (for example crow filler = scared of the light, loses a turn under a flash).
- **Clone batch defects**: several copies of one subject with small numbered defects (one has no eyes = never misses but cannot dodge; one has extra arms). Short batch labels like "Batch 7, unit C".
- **Failure log tie-in**: Archive entries for Sunshine and Nukasourus could be filed as "Rejected: independent thought detected", directly opposite to the enemies' "Approved: full compliance".
- **Presentation (original, not copied)**: a bright "showroom" or open-day broadcast from Fal-125 between waves ("Today's demonstration: compliance under stress"); enemy cards with a green APPROVED stamp; a boss intro like a product launch. Keep it different from World 8's black-and-white tape.
