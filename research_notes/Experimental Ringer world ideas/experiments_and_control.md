# Real experiments and "obedient subject" tropes: inspiration for The Experimental Ringer (World 2)

Research note for FAL125 World 2: Fal-125 counts its mindless, obedient subjects as "successes" and its free-thinking ones as "failures". Sources were found by web search. Wikipedia pages could not be fetched directly (that host was blocked in this session), so the facts below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages. Where sources disagreed, both versions are given.

## Which real experiments are most evocative and suitable (non-gory) for a game?

### Takeaway
The best fits for a "successful = obedient" facility are the experiments about remote control and conditioning: Delgado's bull stopped by a button, RoboRoach steered by its antennae, Skinner's pigeons that steer by majority vote, and the Acoustic Kitty cat that would not obey. Each has a simple, readable mechanic and needs no gore. Demikhov's dogs, Harlow's surrogate mothers, Universe 25 and Laika are strong for mood, but they are sad, so use them as a feeling, not as a joke.

### Cited Findings
**Remote control and brain stimulation**
- Delgado (Yale physiologist) put a "stimoceiver" in a bull at a bull-breeding ranch in Córdoba, Spain. When the bull charged, he pressed a remote button and it stopped. Most sources say 1963; Discover says 1964. — [Wikipedia: José Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Delgado); [Discover: The myth of mind control](https://www.discovermagazine.com/the-myth-of-mind-control-14732)
- After the bull stopped, a second button made it "obediently" turn to the right and trot away. — [Discover](https://www.discovermagazine.com/the-myth-of-mind-control-14732)
- The electrode was in the caudate nucleus (movement). Delgado said the bull lost its aggression, but critics argue it is easier to block movement than motivation. In other words, the bull may not have been calmed, only frozen. — [Wikipedia: Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Delgado)
- Delgado himself dismissed the "Orwellian possibility" and said the technology was too crude for precise mind control. — [Yale Scientific](https://www.yalescientific.org/2011/02/the-brain-machine-connection-humans-and-computers-in-the-21st-century)
- RoboRoach (Backyard Brains, an educational neuroscience company, around 2011-2013) was sold as "the world's first commercially available cyborg": a Bluetooth backpack on a live cockroach, steered from a phone app. — [Make](https://makezine.com/2012/07/24/robo-roach); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/automaton/robotics/diy/remote-control-cyborg-roaches-to-invade-classrooms)
- Stimulating the right antenna makes the roach turn LEFT (it reacts as if it touched a wall). The roach soon gets used to the signal and stops responding (adaptation, noted in Backyard Brains coverage; this detail was not confirmed this session). PETA objected; Backyard Brains said it teaches neuroscience. — [Make](https://makezine.com/2012/07/24/robo-roach); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2013/11/11/roboroach/)
- Backyard Brains' own experiment page now sits under a "retired" path, so the kit may no longer be sold. — [Backyard Brains docs](https://docs.backyardbrains.com/retired/experiments/roboRoachSurgery)

**Conditioning and animal "soldiers"**
- Project Pigeon (B. F. Skinner, WWII): three pigeons in a missile's nose cone pecked at a screen showing the target, and the pecks steered it. The missile moved where two of the three birds agreed (a mechanical vote). — [Wikipedia: Project Pigeon](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Pigeon); [B. F. Skinner Foundation](https://www.bfskinner.org/?p=161)
- It was cancelled (Wikipedia: 8 October 1944; Engadget: funding denied December 1943), then revived by the Navy in 1948 as Project ORCON ("organic control") until 1953. It never saw combat. Skinner received a posthumous Ig Nobel Peace Prize in 2024. — [Wikipedia](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Pigeon); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/hitting-the-books-the-celluloid-specimen-benjamin-schultz-figueroa-university-of-california-press-143028555.html)
- Acoustic Kitty (CIA Directorate of Science & Technology, about 1962-1967): a cat fitted with a microphone, antenna and battery to listen in on Soviet targets. Declassified in 2001. — [Wikipedia: Acoustic Kitty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_Kitty); [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/cia-experimented-animals-1960s-too-just-ask-acoustic-kitty-180964313/)
- Outcome is disputed: one story says the cat was hit by a taxi on its first test; former CIA officer Robert Wallace says it was unharmed and the project was dropped because cats would not follow orders. The cost is given as about $20 million (Victor Marchetti) or $13-15 million. The CIA's own note said the method was "not practical". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_Kitty); [Time](https://time.com/4868642/); [HowStuffWorks](https://history.howstuffworks.com/historical-events/acoustic-kitty.htm)
- US Navy Marine Mammal Program: began around 1959-1960 (first dolphin studied was "Notty"); first aimed at torpedo design, then switched to training dolphins and sea lions. Main jobs: find mines (dolphins use sonar), recover objects (sea lions attach cables), and stop enemy swimmers. Secret for most of its history, revealed in the 1990s. — [Wikipedia: Navy Marine Mammal Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_Marine_Mammal_Program); [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/navy-marine-mammal-program-history-dolphins-sea-lions)
- Deployed in Vietnam (1970), Bahrain (1987) and the Persian Gulf (2003). Size reported as 90 dolphins + 50 sea lions (2015) and 77 dolphins + 47 sea lions (2023 budget). The Navy wants to replace them with drones. — [Navy Times](https://www.navytimes.com/news/your-navy/2015/04/11/sea-lions-dolphins-still-fleet-s-underwater-guardians/); [Yahoo News](https://news.yahoo.com/60-years-us-navy-training-231100452.html)

**Body-building and "made" animals**
- Demikhov (Soviet surgeon) grafted a second dog's head and upper body onto a larger dog, from 1954 on; reportedly 23-24 attempts. The famous pair, Shavka on Brodyaga (photographed by LIFE in 1959), lived about 4 days; the longest pair lived 29 days. One Medium post says 9 days (an outlier). — [Wikipedia: Vladimir Demikhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Demikhov); [Amusing Planet](https://www.amusingplanet.com/2020/06/vladimir-petrovich-demikhovs-two-headed.html); [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/soviet-scientists-made-this-two-headed-dog)
- Demikhov's work is also linked to the history of heart transplants. — [The Courier](https://www.thecourieronline.co.uk/two-headed-dog-the-experiments-of-vladimir-demikhov/)
- Vacanti mouse (Cao, Vacanti et al., 1997, Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery): an ear-shaped biodegradable scaffold seeded with cartilage cells was put under the skin of a hairless mouse; the mouse's blood vessels fed it as it grew into an ear shape. It was never a real ear (no nerves or inner ear), and the aim was to help children born without ears. Sources differ on whether the cells were cow or human (cow is cited most). — [Wikipedia: Vacanti mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacanti_mouse); [Spokesman-Review, 1995](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1995/oct/25/weird-science-mice-grow-ears-tissue-engineering-a/)
- "Ruppy" (Ruby Puppy), 2009: Seoul National University (Lee Byeong-chun) cloned beagles with a sea-anemone fluorescent gene. They look normal in daylight and glow red under UV. Six were born in December 2007; four survived. The aim was disease models (e.g., Parkinson's), not the colour. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna30463427); [Mother Jones](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2009/04/ruppy-glowing-red-beagle-clone/)

**Society and attachment experiments**
- Universe 25 (John B. Calhoun, 1968-1972): a mouse "utopia" with unlimited food, water and nesting for about 3,840 mice, no predators or disease. Started with 8 mice; peaked around 2,200 near day 560, then declined to collapse. — [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/how-mouse-utopias-1960s-led-grim-predictions-humans-180954423/); [Science History Institute](https://sciencehistory.org/distillations/mouse-heaven-or-mouse-hell)
- "The beautiful ones": males who withdrew, never fought, never mated, and only groomed themselves, so they had no scars. — [Science History Institute](https://sciencehistory.org/distillations/mouse-heaven-or-mouse-hell); [Today I Found Out](https://www.todayifoundout.com/index.php/2018/12/that-time-a-guy-tried-to-build-a-utopia-for-mice-and-it-all-went-to-hell/)
- The popular "it predicts human doom" reading is disputed: disease or genetic decline may explain the collapse. — [Gwern: Mouse Utopia](https://gwern.net/mouse-utopia)
- Harlow (rhesus monkeys): infants given a bare wire "mother" with milk and a soft cloth "mother" without it clung to the cloth one, and ran to it when scared. Infants with only a wire mother rocked and clutched themselves. Total isolation caused lasting damage. Now widely seen as unethical. — [APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/obsonline/harlows-classic-studies-revealed-the-importance-of-maternal-contact.html); [University of Oregon](https://pages.uoregon.edu/adoption/studies/HarlowMLE.htm)

**Human programs and secrecy**
- MKUltra (CIA): began 1953, cut back 1964 and 1967, halted 1973. Made public in 1975 (Church Committee, Rockefeller Commission). A 1977 FOIA request found about 20,000 documents, leading to a joint Senate hearing on 3 August 1977. CIA Director Turner described 149 subprojects at 80 institutions. — [CIA-hosted Senate hearing PDF](https://www.cia.gov/library/abbottabad-compound/9D/9D9A27BD7241E2074EAF81C7FC2C86CF_MK.Ultra_Senate.Hearings.pdf); [Wikipedia: MKUltra](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/MKULTRA)
- Laika (Sputnik 2, November 1957): the craft did not separate from its booster and lost insulation, so the cabin overheated. Soviet accounts said she lived days (oxygen ran out, or was put to sleep); in October 2002 Dimitri Malashenkov said she died 5-7 hours after launch from heat and stress. — [Wikipedia: Laika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laika); [Smithsonian Air & Space](https://www.smithsonianmag.com:443/air-space-magazine/laika-declassified-180967077/); [Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/first-death-in-orbit)

### Inferences
- **Best "obedience" stories (light enough for a cute chibi game):** Delgado's button, RoboRoach, Project Pigeon's vote, Acoustic Kitty (a "failure" because it would not obey: that is exactly Fal-125's success/failure rule), and the Navy animals (trained jobs: find, fetch, guard).
- **Best for mood but handle gently:** Demikhov (two heads, one body), Harlow (the wire mother), Universe 25 (paradise that empties), Laika (the official story was a lie), MKUltra (shredded files). These make good lore, Archive entries or boss themes rather than comic enemies.
- **Strong recurring theme across the real cases:** the control was always weaker than it looked (the bull maybe only frozen; roaches adapt; pigeons dropped for electronics; the cat would not obey). That fits World 2 well: the "successes" look perfectly controlled, but the control can slip.
- Careful with names: real people (Delgado, Skinner, Harlow, Calhoun) and real animals (Laika, Ruppy) should not appear by name. Turn them into Fal-125's own versions.

### Gaps
- Pavlov's bell conditioning, "Rat Park" (Bruce Alexander's addiction study), Harlow's later isolation "pit of despair" and chimera research were not searched this session; facts about them are not verified here (Pavlov's dog and bell is widely known).
- The RoboRoach "roach gets used to it in minutes" detail is from memory, not confirmed by a fetched source.
- Exact Demikhov dates and survival counts conflict between sources.

## What obedience/control mechanics appear in fiction and games?

### Takeaway
Fiction makes controlled subjects memorable through (a) a visible classification or rule ("Safe/Euclid/Keter", "the Law"), (b) a bond to a handler or ward that the subject must protect (Big Daddy and Little Sister), (c) a meter that drops on bad treatment and causes a breakout (Lobotomy Corporation's Qliphoth counter), and (d) the reveal that the "safe place" is the experiment (Fallout vaults). All of these are owned ideas: borrow the shape, not the names.

### Cited Findings
- **SCP Foundation** (collaborative online fiction): object classes describe how hard something is to contain, not how dangerous it is. Safe = reliably contained; Euclid = unreliable or poorly understood, and anything that thinks or acts for itself is usually Euclid; Keter = actively resists containment. The fan "box test": put it in a box; nothing happens = Safe, unsure = Euclid, it gets out = Keter. Classes can change (Safe can be raised to Euclid). Extra classes include Thaumiel (used to contain other anomalies), Apollyon, Neutralized, Explained. — [Simple Wikipedia: SCP Foundation](https://simple.wikipedia.com/wiki/SCP_Foundation); [A Little Bit Human guide](https://alittlebithuman.com/a-comprehensive-guide-to-scp-classes-for-new-foundation-recruits/) (fan guides; community consensus)
- **Lobotomy Corporation** (Project Moon game): each "Abnormality" has a Qliphoth counter; bad work results lower it (for example 75-80% chance per bad result), and at zero it may breach containment or trigger a special effect. Four work types: Instinct, Insight, Attachment, Repression; each Abnormality likes some more than others. — [Lobotomy Corporation Wiki](https://lobotomycorp.fandom.com/wiki/ALEPH); [Let's Play Archive](https://lparchive.org/Lobotomy-Corporation/Update%2073); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Videogame/LobotomyCorporation)
- **BioShock** (2K/Irrational): Big Daddies are guards bonded to Little Sisters. In BioShock 2, Subject Delta is the first one "pair-bonded" to a single Little Sister; straying too far causes coma or madness. A "Hypnotize Big Daddy" plasmid in the first game makes one protect the player and abandon its ward. — [Wikipedia: Big Daddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Daddy_(BioShock)); [BioShock Wiki](https://bioshock.fandom.com/wiki/BioShock_2); [Lot 255](https://breezewiki.discard.no/bioshock/wiki/Lot_255)
- **Fallout** (Bethesda/Interplay): Vault-Tec's "Societal Preservation Program" turned many shelters into secret social experiments, with a few "control" vaults left normal for comparison (Vault 13, Vault 8; 17 of 122 known vaults by one count). Examples: Vault 12's door built not to close; Vault 101's Overseer with total power; Vault 75 breeding soldiers until the subjects revolted. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/fallout-vault-history); [GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/culture/the-most-disturbing-vault-tec-experiments-in-fallout-ranked/)
- **The Island of Doctor Moreau** (H. G. Wells, 1896): the Beast Folk chant "the Law" ("Not to go on all-fours; that is the Law. Are we not Men?") led by the Sayer of the Law; over time they slip back to animal habits. — [Standard Ebooks, ch. 12](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/h-g-wells/the-island-of-doctor-moreau/text/chapter-12); [Wikipedia: The Island of Doctor Moreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Island_of_Doctor_Moreau) (public domain novel, so the idea of a chanted Law is free to echo; the exact chant is famous and should not be copied)

### Inferences
- What makes these memorable: a **label the player can read**, a **rule the player can break**, and a **moment the control fails**. FAL125 already has containment levels in its Character Book, so a class-like label on World 2 enemies fits naturally, but use Fal-125's own words, not "Safe/Euclid/Keter".
- Not searched this session (described from general knowledge, so verify before relying): Half-Life (Black Mesa scientists, the G-Man; the Combine's Stalkers are humans turned into mindless workers), Resident Evil (Umbrella's Tyrants follow orders; Nemesis is controlled by a parasite), Portal (GLaDOS's polite test-chamber voice and "the cake"). These ideas belong to those works; borrow only the general shape (an announcer voice, tests with fake rewards, a guard that follows orders).

### Gaps
- No pages for Half-Life, Resident Evil or Portal were checked this session.
- The FAL125 W8 rules already say to avoid quoting The Mandela Catalogue; the same care applies here: no names, chants or slogans from these works.

## What enemy concepts naturally fall out of these?

### Takeaway
Most ideas become one of four turn-based shapes: (1) **commanded units** that only act when a handler gives an order, (2) **conditioned triggers** that react to a cue, (3) **obedience meters** where a "success" can tip into a free-thinking "failure" (stronger, unpredictable), and (4) **stuck-together or tested bodies**. These are Claude's own design ideas, based on the facts above; none of them are taken from an existing game unless marked.

### Cited Findings
- Mechanics below are drawn from the facts in sections 1 and 2 (Delgado's button, Skinner's 2-of-3 vote, RoboRoach's opposite turn, the cat that would not obey, Navy find/fetch/guard jobs, Harlow's wire vs cloth, Universe 25's beautiful ones, Laika's false report, MKUltra's files). See those sections for sources.

### Inferences (enemy, boss and atmosphere ideas; all original drafts, names are placeholders)
**World rule idea (like WSP in V269):** *"Compliance"*: every World 2 enemy has a small Compliance number (0-3 pips). While it has pips it obeys its handler or script exactly (predictable). Effects that stun, confuse or "talk" to it remove pips; at 0 it becomes a **Failure**: it stops following orders, gets +ATK, and picks random targets (including other enemies). Cuts both ways, like the other world rules: breaking them makes them dangerous but also makes them hit their own side.

**Commanded units (handler idea, from Delgado and the Navy):**
- *Handler + Subject pair*: a weak Handler with a remote. The Subject skips its turn unless the Handler used "Command" that round. Kill the Handler and the Subject either freezes (Success) or goes wild (Failure, see rule above). Players must choose.
- *The Button*: a charging brute that winds up for one turn; a hidden "Stop" fires if any enemy Handler is alive, cancelling the charge. Echo of the bull, with no named person.
- *Retriever*: fetches a downed enemy back to the fight (sea-lion "recover" job). *Sentry*: blocks the first hit on its ward each turn ("swimmer interdiction" turned into a guard).

**Conditioned triggers (Skinner, Pavlov, RoboRoach):**
- *The Bell*: a ringer object on the field (fits the world name "Ringer"). When it rings, every Subject acts at once. Players can break the bell, or hit the bell themselves to make enemies waste a turn.
- *Flock of three*: a 3-head bird unit; it attacks the target at least 2 heads "voted" for (show the votes). Taunting one head changes the vote.
- *Reverse Steer*: a cyborg insect; when you buff or target it, it moves to the OTHER side (swaps target). It adapts: the effect stops after 2 uses.
- *Reward Light*: an enemy that only attacks when it was "rewarded" (healed or buffed by an ally) last turn; cutting its rewards makes it idle.

**Obedience meter / success vs failure (core theme):**
- *The Beautiful One*: spotless, never attacks, very high DEF, and slowly buffs itself. Harmless but blocks waves; Fal-125 calls it a perfect success. A nice dark joke.
- *Escaped Failure* (mini-boss): a subject that "thinks", taunts the player, and sometimes helps them; Fal-125 sends Successes to retake it. Could also hint at a future recruit.
- *Unfit Spy* (cat-like): ignores orders 50% of the time; when it ignores them it wanders and attacks whoever is lowest HP on either side.

**Built and tested bodies (Demikhov, Vacanti, Ruppy, Harlow):**
- *Two-Headed Hound*: two heads share one HP bar but take two turns; each head obeys a different command, so they sometimes argue (skip). Keep it cute, no stitches or blood.
- *Grafted Carrier*: carries an extra growing part on its back that grows a stack each turn and bursts into a heal for allies; hit the growth to stop it.
- *Glow Pup*: looks normal; under "UV" (a light status, could link with W3 Searchlight) it reveals its true type and weakness.
- *Wire Mother / Cloth Mother* (boss pair): Wire feeds (heals) its children but gives no defense; Cloth gives defense but no heal. Kill one and the young units cling to the other. Gentle but haunting.

**Bosses and big ideas:**
- *The Utopia* (world boss): an enclosure full of food; each round more "residents" appear, but after a peak they stop breeding and turn into Beautiful Ones. Survive the crowd and the fight ends by itself (callback to Universe 25 without naming it).
- *The Overseer / Director*: Fal-125's voice in the world. Gives "Directives" to Subjects (link with W5 Directive rule already in the game, so avoid overlap or reuse it deliberately).
- *The Capsule*: an animal sent somewhere it cannot come back from. A sad, quiet boss whose "official report" text lies about how long it survived. Better as an Archive lore piece than a fight.

**Atmosphere ideas:**
- Test-result stamps on defeated enemies: "SUCCESS" (did as told) or "FAILURE" (broke). Shredded or redacted file notes in the Archive (MKUltra feel, without real names).
- Background sounds: bells, clickers, a buzzer; signs like "Reward pending", "Subject compliant".
- Wave names as trial numbers (Trial 23 of 24, like Demikhov's attempts, without saying so).
- Possible link to canon: the "Ringer" name could mean the bell that rings commands, and the people who are put "through the ringer".

**Originality flags:** Qliphoth-style breakout meter (Lobotomy Corporation), pair-bonded guardian and ward (BioShock), "control vault" and Overseer (Fallout), "Safe/Euclid/Keter" (SCP), chanted Law (Moreau), test-chamber announcer with fake rewards (Portal). Use only the general shape, and Fal-125's own words.
