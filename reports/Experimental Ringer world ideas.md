# Build The Ringer around obedient "successes"

**The best version of "The Experimental Ringer" is a bright, clean showroom world where Fal-125 shows off its "successful" experiments: animals, humans and revived dinosaurs that do exactly what they are told.** The horror is not blood. The horror is how empty and obedient they are. Sunshine and Nukasaurus are the opposite: the "failures" who think. This gives the world a clear heart. The game has no dinosaur, lizard or "obedient subject" enemy yet, so the space is free, and nothing in the game is called "Ringer" (only the healer build "Stringer"). The world also pairs well with the Sewer, which already says "Everything Fal-125 threw away ended up here" (game text, V272 notes). The Ringer becomes where the experiments that worked are kept, and the Sewer where the failures were dumped. I suggest the world rule **"The Bell"**: a bell rings every few rounds and all collared enemies act together, but a broken collar makes an enemy wild. This is new for the game, cuts both ways, and does not repeat the Alarm, Tide, Searchlight, Hospitality or Directive. This report gives about 40 enemy ideas, 6 floor bosses with phases, look and music ideas, shop and Archive touches, and how Sunshine and Nukasaurus react. It also explains the **build risk**: the game finds worlds by number, so inserting a world in second place touches about 30 places and the save file. The safest path is to build it first without moving anything, then do one careful insert with a save fix. Everything below is a draft idea, not canon. Ideas that echo other works are marked **[Inspired by ...]**.

## What "Ringer" can mean, and the story it tells

"Ringer" has three meanings, and the game can use all three at once. A **fighting ring** gives the arena. A **bell** gives the world rule. A **look-alike copy** (a "dead ringer") gives the creepy secret: the experiments look like the real animals, but they are copies. This last meaning has real science behind it. In 2025 a company edited 20 places in 14 genes of a gray wolf and called the pups "dire wolves"; its own chief scientist later said "Our animals are gray wolves" ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/animals/extinct-species/our-animals-are-gray-wolves-colossal-didnt-de-extinct-dire-wolves-chief-scientist-clarifies)), and critics said they only look like the extinct animal ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-scientific-debate-over-colossals-de-extinct-dire-wolves/)). That gives Fal-125 a perfect line for the Archive: its "dinosaurs" are lizards, birds and crocodiles edited to look the part. Sunshine and Nukasaurus are the same kind of lizard, but their copies "went wrong" by thinking.

Real history also fits the "success" idea. In every famous control experiment, the control was weaker than it looked. A bull stopped by a remote button may only have been frozen, not calmed ([Wikipedia: José Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Delgado)). A cat fitted with a listening device was dropped partly because cats would not follow orders ([Wikipedia: Acoustic Kitty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_Kitty)). Three pigeons in a missile steered it by majority vote ([Wikipedia: Project Pigeon](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Pigeon)). So the story can say: Fal-125 thinks the control is perfect, but the player can find the crack.

**Story beats (3 scenes, matching the game's start / middle / end pattern):**

- **Start, "The Intake Gate":** After the Warden falls, the reactor vents down. But the party first lands in a clean hall with a cheerful voice: "Welcome to the showcase." The party sees rows of calm animals and people standing still. Eyianix notices nobody is afraid, which is different from World 1, where subjects were "terrified".
- **Middle, "The Demonstration Floor":** The party sees a staged show: a guard dog, a person and a dinosaur all obey one bell. Radiant realises the "success" label means "no thoughts left". A hidden note (a staff member's) says two subjects were rejected for "independent thought" (Sunshine and Nukasaurus).
- **End, "The Ring":** The final boss is Fal-125's best showpiece. After it falls, the floor opens to the drain pipes. The exit line replaces the current hand-off ("whatever this place throws away ends up in the sewers"): the Ringer's rejected files lead down to the Sewer.

## Name ideas and the world rule: The Bell

**Name options (all say "Ringer" in a different way):**

- **The Experimental Ringer** (your title): fighting ring, bell and copy all at once.
- **Ringer Hall**: sounds like a showroom.
- **The Showcase Ring**: puts the "look what we made" feeling first.
- **The Bell Room**: calm and eerie.

**The main rule, "The Bell" (recommended).** Every enemy has a **Collar**. Every 3rd round a bell rings, and all collared enemies get a free, perfectly timed action together (a group strike or group shield). The bell shows one round ahead ("Bell in 1"), so the player can plan. Cutting a collar needs one strong hit (or a stun) on that enemy. A broken enemy is **Wild**: it ignores the bell, hits harder (+ATK), but picks random targets, even its own side. So it is good (it stops the group strike and may hit its friends) and bad (it hits you harder, and unpredictably). The same idea is "break the collar early (lose the telegraphs) or fight the obedient but organised version" ([game design note, Claude draft]). **[Inspired by: collars and remote control in real experiments (the stopped bull) and in many games; the collar-break-gives-wild trick is a draft, not copied.]**

Important: World 2 (the Sewer, soon World 3) already has the Tide, which fires every 3rd round. The Bell should **not** also be every 3rd round if the Tide stays on the Sewer, but the Sewer will be World 3, and the Tide rule belongs to the Sewer, not to the Ringer. Ask the author whether the Tide moves to the Sewer's new number (my advice: yes, it keeps its place and the Ringer gets a new rule). I suggest the Bell rings every **4th** round to keep them different in rhythm.

**Other two-sided rule ideas (to use as floor twists, boss rules, or backups):**

| Rule | How it works (good side / bad side) |
|---|---|
| Test Score | A score fills when you do "interesting" things (different fighter each turn, quick kills). High score gives a reward, but the lab adds an enemy next wave. Low score is easy but pays nothing. **[Inspired by: a testing voice that grades you, as in Portal-style games. The score itself is generic.]** |
| Breach Meter | Each kill fills a meter. When full, a cage opens and a caged dinosaur enters and attacks everyone, enemies too. It can clear the wave or wipe you. **[Inspired by: breakout counters in Lobotomy Corporation ([wiki](https://lobotomycorp.fandom.com/wiki/Dimensional_Refraction_Variant)). Keep the look and rules your own.]** |
| Feeding Time | Every few rounds food drops on the lowest-HP unit on each side. Carnivores hit that unit harder, but the unit is healed. Not taken from a specific game. |
| Cage Doors | Side doors open and close each round. A fighter by an open door is exposed to a caged beast, but also gets a free swing at it. Shown one round ahead. |
| Obedience Pips | Each enemy has 0-3 "compliance" pips. Stuns and talk effects remove pips. At 0 it becomes a Failure: stronger, random targets. This is a softer cousin of the Bell and could be the rule inside the Bell. |
| Rehearsal | Enemies repeat the exact order of the last round (shown with arrows). If you break the pattern (kill the leader), they lose a turn. |
| Judges' Card | Each wave has a "judge" at the back that scores your team. If the team uses the same move 3 times, the judge changes the enemy to resist it. Careful: the W7 cult already adapts to your team, so this must be different (per move, not per team). |

My recommendation: **The Bell as the main rule, Obedience Pips as its engine, Breach Meter as the boss-floor twist.** Avoid Sedative Gas (copies the W1 Alarm's "drag on and trouble comes") and Power Outage (too close to the Searchlight), as the design notes warn.

## Floors: five rooms, one clear story

The game's shape is 5 waves per floor and 5 floors, with a boss on wave 5 of each floor (existing worlds, game notes). I suggest 5 floors with 5 bosses (the author asked for 5-6, so a 6th floor is optional).

| Floor | Name | Feeling | Special touch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Intake Gate** | Clean, bright, calm. Small animals and ordinary people stand in rows. | Teaches the Bell gently. The voice says "Welcome." |
| 2 | **The Demonstration Floor** | A stage with seats for staff. Waves are called "Trials". | Test scores shown after each wave ("Trial 2-3: PASS"). |
| 3 | **The Nursery** | Warm lights, cribs and egg racks. Hatchlings and nurses. | First revived dinosaurs. The voice starts to glitch. |
| 4 | **The Breeding Yard** | Big fences and feeding troughs, half outdoors under a fake sky. | The "breach" floor. Cages fail. The voice says everything is fine. |
| 5 | **The Ring** | A round pit with a crowd of still subjects. | Final boss and the exit down to the Sewer. |
| 6 (optional) | **The Back Office** | Dull rooms of files, lunch tables, staff who do not look up. | A short secret floor with Sunshine/Nukasaurus lore. |

**Floor structure idea from real games:** Lobotomy Corporation escalates in named tiers tied to danger level ([wiki: Ordeals](https://lobotomycorp.fandom.com/wiki/Ordeals)), and a floor-by-floor "calm voice, then the voice breaks" pattern is a standard way to tell a facility story. Use your own names (the table above), not Dawn/Noon/Dusk/Midnight.

## Six boss ideas with phases

The game gives each boss an automatic phase 2 at half health (+25% ATK, a summoned helper), and finals get guards and a final surge (game notes). These boss ideas fit that. A short tell (a warning icon one turn ahead) suits them.

1. **The Usher Captain (Floor 1)**: a human with a bell. Phase 1: rings the bell every second round, and all small enemies act together. Phase 2: the bell cracks; one collared enemy goes wild each round. Teaches the Bell.
2. **The Referee (Floor 2)**: a human in a striped coat. Phase 1: whistles to block a fighter's best move. Phase 2: calls "Trial Over" and removes the buffs from both sides. Teaches the Test Score if you choose that rule.
3. **Hatchery Matron (Floor 3)**: a tall nurse-type with a trolley of eggs. Phase 1: hatchlings join one by one. Phase 2: she picks up a hatchling and shields it; hit the hatchling to hurt her. **[Inspired by: bonded guardian and ward pairs in BioShock-style games; keep it your own.]**
4. **The Wire Mother and the Cloth Mother (Floor 4, a pair)**: one feeds her children (heals) but has no defence; the other defends but cannot heal. Kill one and the young cling to the other. Gentle and haunting. **[Inspired by: the famous monkey comfort study ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/obsonline/harlows-classic-studies-revealed-the-importance-of-maternal-contact.html)); use no names, and no cruelty on screen.]**
5. **The Collared Apex (Floor 5, final)**: the biggest revived dinosaur with a handler. Phase 1: the handler shows its next move ("Next: TAIL SWEEP all"). Phase 2: the collar breaks; no warnings, but it hits its own handler and adds. Phase 3 at 25%: "Revive-with-pause": it falls, stays down one turn while the lab pumps it with serum (a free setup turn for you), and returns in a new form. **[Inspired by: stun-and-revive boss phases in Slay the Spire-style games ([Neon Lights Media](https://www.neonlightsmedia.com/blog/slay-the-spire-2-test-subject-boss-guide)).]**
6. **The Utopia (optional 6th, a secret fight)**: an enclosure full of food. Each round more residents appear; after a peak they stop breeding and turn into harmless "Beautiful Ones", and the fight ends by itself. **[Inspired by: a famous mouse-utopia study that peaked at about 2,200 and then fell ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/how-mouse-utopias-1960s-led-grim-predictions-humans-180954423/)); the real meaning is disputed, so treat it as a mood.]**

**Boss helpers and tell tips:** the final needs a guard (the game gives the finals an opening guard) and one "tell" type. A final HP number should sit between the Warden (1750) and the Drowned King (1500) or the Sewer final should be raised. This was not balance-tested.

## Look, sound and mood

**Look:** Make this world **bright and clean**, not dark. White tile, soft green lights, rows of identical chairs, "APPROVED" stamps in green. This is the opposite of World 8's grey broken tape, so the two do not blur. Staff wear pale uniforms with a number on the sleeve. The horror is that nothing is out of place.

**The showroom voice:** an announcer between waves with cheerful lines like "Today's demonstration: calm under stress." Write your own lines. The calm-voice-over-horror trick is a standard in analog horror ([Dread Central](https://dreadcentral.com/editorials/439064/analog-horror-and-its-blueprints-for-horrors-future)), and cheerful "educational tape" framing is a known way to make something feel wrong ([F Newsmagazine](https://fnewsmagazine.com/2022/02/ghosts-in-the-machine-archiving-the-end-of-the-world-with-gemini-home-entertainment/)). Do not copy the names, groups or lines of any dinosaur analog-horror series.

**The Bell as sound:** a soft chime before each Bell round, a deep bong when it rings. When a collar breaks, the chime goes flat. Floors 3-4 add a nursery music box and heavy footsteps heard a turn before a big dinosaur arrives. Hearing a dinosaur before seeing it is a classic way to build tension ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/dino-crisis-review/1900-2547330/)).

**Music (the game needs a floor song and boss song per floor):** Floor 1 a calm elevator-style tune; Floor 2 a game-show beat; Floor 3 a slow music box; Floor 4 a low rumble with bells; Floor 5 a march with drums. Phase 2 can use the faster "hot" version of the boss song, which the game supports. Loudness should be checked in the browser.

## How Sunshine and Nukasaurus react

Current game text says Nukasaurus is "Fal-125's attempt to make new life from old dinosaur DNA" and that it "sort of worked" ("Sort of" is the Archive's answer); almost all of this, and all of Sunshine's text, is marked as Claude's guess in the Character Book (V272 notes). So your new canon (both are lizards given dinosaur DNA, failures because they think) replaces guesses, not your own writing. Nukasaurus does not talk in story scenes (narration only), so its reactions below should be narration or a look, not speech.

**Sunshine (talks, quiet, ambusher):**
- On Floor 1: "They stand so still. I used to stand like that." Then: "Don't be nice to them. They won't know."
- On a wild (collar-broken) enemy: she is the only one who watches it closely and says, "Let it leave."
- Special: when she hits a collared enemy first (her ambush), the collar cracks more easily. This matches her passive (hits harder on an enemy that has not acted yet).
- A rare line near the end: "They gave me a number. I gave it back."

**Nukasaurus (narration only):**
- On the Harvester and the Herald: it looks at them as if at family, and a small green glow fades.
- Narration: "The big lizard does not roar at the revived dinosaurs. It sits down in front of them, and waits." They do not move.
- At the boss: it steps between the party and the Collared Apex. If it is in your team, the final boss has a short extra line.
- Quirk idea: "Failure Pride" in this world (a small boost, since the lab considers it a failure but it is free). The game already gives world quirks to fighters (Born Here in W1, Property Recall in W5), so this fits.

**Existing recruit lines:** both have Sewer recruit lines in the game ("A shaft of daylight falls through a manhole"). If the Ringer becomes World 2, these would show in the Ringer unless moved or kept. A good fix: they appear *at the exit of the Ringer* (hand-off to the Sewer) which makes story sense if you want them recruited in the Sewer. Another option: recruit them in the Ringer, since they are escaped Ringer failures. The author should choose (see questions).

**Link to the Archive:** their Archive card gets a new pair of lines: Sunshine's says "Rejected: independent thought detected"; Nukasaurus's keeps its "Sort of" and adds "Compliance: 12%".

## Build risk: inserting a world in second place

**The short version:** The game finds almost every world by its **position in a list** (World 1 is 0, the Sewer is 1, and so on). Only World 8 is found by name. If the Ringer goes in position 1, **every later world moves by one**, and any rule that says "if world is 6, do the cult thing" now points to the wrong world. This is a large change with many places to fix. The research found **about 30 places** plus the save file.

**What would break if you only add the world and nothing else:**

| Area | What goes wrong |
|---|---|
| World rules and quirks | Lists by number (healing strength per world, fighter quirks per world, drowsy effects, the five world specials) would give the Sewer the Ringer's numbers and everyone after it the wrong ones. |
| The cult world | About 10 checks say "world 6 = The Cult". After the insert, the Cult would be 7 and World 7's rules would hit the wrong world. |
| Route text and backgrounds | Route flavour and floor backgrounds are keyed by number. The Sewer would show the wrong scenes. |
| Story scenes | The story script is a list by number. Echo scenes in later worlds read the "previous world's record" and would read the wrong one. |
| Recruiting | Custom recruit lines show only in the first two worlds; they would show in the wrong world. |
| Lists of worlds | Archive pool, boss rush, endless mode, daily worlds, story-choice records, and trophies are all lists of numbers. |
| Incidents | Each incident lists a world number (for example the Sewer incidents). They would point at the Ringer. |
| Music and scenes | Each world needs a floor song, boss song, final song, theme and a drawn backdrop per floor. |
| Test tools | The lab and enemy-move tools count worlds 1-7. |
| **The save file** | "Highest world unlocked", story choices, endless bests, Lorebook visits and the saved selected world are all stored by number. **Old saves would point to the wrong worlds.** Players who finished World 5 might suddenly see World 5 as the Safe Haven again, or lose progress. |

**Why the save file matters most:** the project rule is "do not break old saves." A player on World 3 today would, after the insert, have "highest world = 2" and would see the Ringer and Sewer as open but the old World 3 as locked, unless a one-time fix moves old values.

### The safest way, step by step

I recommend a **three-stage plan**. This keeps the game working at every step.

1. **Stage 1: build the Ringer without moving anything.** Add the world at the **end** of the list (like the Casino and Clown side quests, which already sit after the main worlds) and mark it hidden. Build all enemies, bosses, rules, music, art and story for it. Test it fully in the lab. Nothing in the real game changes, and no save is touched. This is the biggest part of the work and carries no risk.
2. **Stage 2: add a "world number" helper before the insert.** Give each world a name key (as World 8 already has) and replace the number checks with name checks, one world at a time, running the tests after each. Keep the old slot names for the story lines ("w2s", "w3s" and so on) so the roughly 120 fighters' line tables are not renamed; give the Ringer **new** slot names (for example "r2s", "r2m", "r2e") instead. This stage reduces risk for any future world too.
3. **Stage 3: do the insert and a one-time save fix in one version.** Move the Ringer to position 1, and run a save fix that adds 1 to every saved world number that was 1 or higher (highest unlocked, story choices, endless bests, Lorebook visits, and the saved world choice). Mark the save as "fixed" so it only runs once. Back up the save format in the README. Then run **all** tests: every character test, the world specials, world 8, the story casts, the lines audit, the enemy-move tool, and a balance run. Finally play one battle in a browser in every world, with no console errors, and on a phone-sized window.

**Other safe-ish option (not recommended):** keep the Ringer at the end of the list and add a display layer that shows it as "World 2". It avoids shifting numbers, but the display layer must be applied everywhere the world number is shown or compared. The research judged this likely **more fragile** than a clean insert, because there are so many "world equals N" tests.

**Balance:** the Ringer's difficulty sits between World 1 (1.0 to 1.7 scaling) and the Sewer (1.2 to 1.95). The Sewer's scaling may need to move up a little, and the Sewer's clear rates should be measured again. The balance tools exist; run the early test (60 runs) and trust it over the late test, which is noisy.

**Name clash warning:** do not reuse these names: Handler, Returned Subject, The Culled, Prototype Zero, Guard Drone, Nurse, Warden's Hound. The Ringer can link to them in the story instead.

## Conclusion

The strongest new insight is that the world name, rule, story and cast all fit together: **a bell that commands, collars that can be cut, a showroom that celebrates emptiness, and two escaped failures who are the only ones with a will.** The real experiments show the same pattern: the control always looked stronger than it was. That is the player's way in: cut the collar, and the obedient enemy becomes wild.

The biggest practical point is that the risky part is not the ideas but the **insert**. Build the Ringer first as a hidden world, then do the move with one save fix. The author has said new characters will soon stop, so a world like this is also a good way to spend the remaining effort on something that does not need any new fighters at all.
